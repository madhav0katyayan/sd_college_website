About SDCET

A distinctive combination of academic excellence & professional ethics

Established in 1997 on Jansath Road, Muzaffarnagar, S.D. College of Engineering & Technology has spent over 25 years building career aspirants into industry-ready engineers and managers. Approved by AICTE and affiliated to Dr. A.P.J. Abdul Kalam Technical University, Lucknow (AKTU code –083), SDCET was the first NBA-accredited institute in the region — a distinction that continues to define how the college teaches, mentors, and places its students.

Our classrooms carry mentorship from IIT and internationally-qualified professors, backed by a central library, computer centres, and dedicated learning-resource facilities across a lush green campus.

Vision & Mission

What we stand for

The principles that guide how SDCET teaches, mentors and prepares every student for a career in engineering and management.

Our Vision

To develop students who can sustain the rigorous industrial requirements in terms of technical skills and motivation, and to establish strong linkages with industries and other research organizations of national and international repute.

Our Mission

  • To establish the institute as an institute of excellence.
  • To achieve excellence in imparting technical and professional education.
  • To strive to inculcate a sense of integrity and a high sense of responsibility amongst students.
  • To produce technically skilled manpower who can contribute towards the growth of the region as well as the nation.
Quality Policy & Objective

Our commitment to quality

S.D. College of Engineering & Technology is committed to providing quality technical education through continuous improvement in teaching-learning processes, infrastructure and student support services, in line with AICTE and AKTU norms.

01

Maintain high academic standards through regular curriculum review and faculty development.

02

Ensure adequate infrastructure, laboratories and learning resources for effective teaching-learning.

03

Promote student-centric practices including mentoring, feedback and grievance redressal.

04

Strengthen industry-institute interaction for internships, projects and placements.

05

Continuously monitor and improve academic and administrative processes through IQAC.

06

Encourage research, innovation and entrepreneurship across all departments.

Code of Conduct

Code of Conduct for Students

01

All students admitted to the Institute are subject to the discipline and control of the Institute authorities. The students are required to adhere strictly to the rules and regulations that may be framed from time to time by the Institute authorities.

02

Proper observance of Institute’s discipline, good conduct and participation in Curricular, Co-Curricular, and Extracurricular activities are important for successful graduation and a bright future of the students.

03

Students must be regular and punctual in attending the classes and all other activities of the Institute. It must be ensured that the students maintain at least the minimum 75% attendance as specified by the University norms.

04

Each student must wear Institute uniform on the days as instructed by the Institute. Students shall come to the campus clean, tidy and neatly dressed.

05

Students must carry his/her identity card while in the campus and also outside the campus while representing the Institute.

06

A student must take prior written permission of the Class Mentor and the HOD in case he/she is unable to attend the lectures and/or practicals for more than three days.

07

Students should help to maintain the campus clean, tidy and plastic free.

08

The conduct of the students inside and outside the campus should be such that it will raise their own image and the image of the Institute in society.

09

The students must not participate in any antisocial activity. If any objectionable conduct within or outside the premises of the Institute by the student is observed or reported, then he/she is liable for strict disciplinary action.

10

No notice of any kind shall be circulated among the students or pasted on the notice-board without the previous written approval from the Director.

11

According to the verdict of the Supreme Court and the University Act 1956–Regulation 26(1)g–2009, the students found guilty of ragging are liable for punishment.

12

Students should read the notices put on the notice board of the Institute, department and office regularly.

Acts that Constitute Misconduct — Leading to Disciplinary Action

01

Physical assault or threat, against any member of the campus.

02

Carrying of, use of or threat to use any weapons.

03

Violation of the status, dignity and honor of a student belonging to the Scheduled Castes, Scheduled Tribes and/or any religion.

04

Any practice, whether verbal or otherwise, derogatory to girls/women.

05

Creating ill-will or intolerance on religious or communal grounds.

06

Disrupting any teaching, study, assessment or research activities or the administration of the institute.

07

Damaging or wrongfully dealing with any property under the control of the Institute; any property on Institute premises; or property on a location where a student is present under the auspices of the Institute.

08

Withholding relevant information or furnishing false or misleading information for purposes connected with academic progression.

09

Misuse of social media for spreading/forwarding contents that are objectionable by law.

10

Indulging in any antisocial activity.
